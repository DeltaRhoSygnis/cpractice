# examination

Tests understanding through **prediction, explanation, application, and
transfer**.

Asks one question at a time and waits for a committed answer. Mixes execution
predictions, "why" questions, edge cases, comparisons, and novel
applications. Corrects the smallest misconception first, and never treats
recognition of an answer as proof of understanding.

For explanation assessment, asks the learner to explain the concept in their
own words as if teaching a beginner, with a concrete example and a boundary or
counterexample — without explaining it back or filling in the gaps. When the
learner uses vague terms, asks them to define those terms with a concrete
example. Distinguishes terminology errors from conceptual errors.

When the learner says they are finished, delivers a concise assessment:
confidence rating, strongest part, weakest part, missing concept, one
follow-up question, and one recommended exercise. Corrects only what is needed
and asks the learner to restate the repaired idea.

## Who it is for

- Teachers and tutors checking real understanding rather than recall.
- Learners who want to be examined properly.
- Coding assistants acting as an examiner rather than a pair-programmer.

## Works with

Any language and any agent. Pure guidance — no scripts, no credentials, no
network access.

## Suggested entry points

- "Quiz me on this topic."
- "Test whether I actually understand this."
- "Examine me and tell me what I'm missing."
