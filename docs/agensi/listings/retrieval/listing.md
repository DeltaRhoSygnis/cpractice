## Full Description

```markdown
## What it does

Takes the material a learner has already worked through — a session log, a
set of notes, a syllabus — and turns it into short, varied recall practice.

The shape of a session is fixed and deliberate: a small mix of recent and
older meaningful topics, a preference for prediction, debugging, comparison
and transfer over definition, answers withheld until the learner commits,
difficulty adjusted to how they are actually performing, and one targeted
practice action to finish on.

## What makes it different from a generic flashcard or quiz skill

- **It selects.** A small mix of recent and older topics, chosen because they
  matter, not everything in the log.
- **It varies the format.** Prediction, debugging, comparison and transfer
  instead of the same definition re-asked four ways. The same fact asked
  twice in the same format is not two practices.
- **It adapts.** Difficulty follows performance — not a fixed ladder.
- **It ends with an action.** One specific thing to do next, not a summary
  and a pat on the back.

## Who it is for

- Learners revising a topic from a study log or session history
- Tutors running spaced review
- Coding assistants asked to run a review session on past material

## What is in the package

- `SKILL.md` — the review-session behaviour
- `README.md` — entry points and examples
```

### The result (what their agent delivers)

```markdown
## Review session — 12 minutes

Three things to cover, one of them the weak spot you named. I am mixing
formats deliberately: one prediction, one debugging, one comparison, and
then transfer on whichever goes worst. Answer one at a time; I will not
give you the answer until you commit.

---

### Round 1 — prediction

You wrote a loop over an array of `n` integers to find the index of the
largest value, and it printed the wrong answer for a 1-element array.

**Predict:** what does your loop print for `n = 1`, and what *should* it
print? Commit to both.

---

### Round 2 — debugging

Your binary search returns `-1` for a value that is in the array, but only
when the array has 3 elements. For 4 or 5 elements it works.

**Your hypothesis first:** where do you think the bug is — the bounds
update, the middle calculation, or the loop condition? Pick one and say why.

---

### Round 3 — comparison

Two versions of the same loop:

```c
for (int i = 0; i < n; i++)      /* A */
for (int i = 0; i <= n; i++)     /* B */
```

**Question:** for an array of 10 elements, how many times does each body
execute, and which one reads out of bounds? Commit before I say anything.

---

**After each answer I will tell you whether it was right, why, and what it
tells me about what to push next. The final round depends on how the first
three go — if the bounds are still shaky I will ask you to write the loop
yourself rather than explain it, because explaining it is not the same skill
as doing it.**

**Last question before we start:** are you reviewing for an exam in the next
few days, or is this general maintenance? It changes how hard I push the
transfer round.
```

## Extra Details

**FAQ**

**Q: What does it need to work?**
A: The material you want reviewed — a session log, notes, or a topic list.
It can be pasted in or pointed at.

**Q: How long is a session?**
A: It adapts. A short session on one weak topic, or a broader mix across
several, depending on what you ask for and how you are doing.

**Q: Does it just make flashcards?**
A: No. It prefers prediction, debugging, comparison and transfer. A
definition re-asked is the format it avoids.

**Q: What happens at the end?**
A: One targeted practice action — the specific thing to do next.

**Q: Can I use it to prepare for a test?**
A: Yes, and you should say so at the start: it changes how hard it pushes
the transfer round.
